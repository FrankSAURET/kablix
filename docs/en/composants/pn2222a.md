# Transistor PN2222A (NPN)

![Transistor PN2222A](../../img/composants/pn2222a.webp)

Small-signal NPN bipolar transistor in a TO-92 package. In Kablix it acts as a **commanded switch**: a base current lets through a collector current up to 35 times larger.

## Pins

| Pin | Role |
|--------|------|
| **E** | Emitter — tied to ground in the classic circuit |
| **B** | Base — control, ALWAYS behind a resistor |
| **C** | Collector — the load being switched (relay, motor, LED) |

## Properties

| Property | Role | Default |
|-----------|------|--------|
| `angle` | Orientation (0/90/180/270°) | 0 |

The PN2222A is a fixed reference: gain 35, max Vce 40 V, max Ic 600 mA. To pick those values yourself, use the **generic NPN transistor** instead.

## Simulation

- The transistor conducts when its **base is high and its emitter low**.
- The current it passes is capped at **Gain × Ib** (35 × Ib): that is the whole model. So aim for **saturation** — if the downstream circuit needs more, it does not work (the fan will not start, the relay will not pull in).
- A base wired **without a resistor** always saturates… and would cook a real transistor: add a base resistor.

## Usage

- Typical figures: to drive a 40 mA relay coil you need Ib ≥ 40 / 35 ≈ 1.2 mA. At 5 V a 1 kΩ base resistor gives (5 − 0.7) / 1000 ≈ 4.3 mA: well saturated.
- Base resistor too large (100 kΩ) → Ib = 43 µA → max Ic ≈ 1.5 mA: the load never starts. That is the classic mistake to watch for in simulation.
- With a relay the **flyback diode is mandatory** (cathode towards +): without it the switch-off spike would destroy the transistor.
